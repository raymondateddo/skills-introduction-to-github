##Answer key [Answer-key-link](drive link) 

Q1. What causes seasonal temperature variation between Wombat season (July) and Biderap season (January) in Australia?

Student explanations should include:
 When it is Wombat season (July), the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun.
Since the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun, they would experience lower amounts of daylight and longer amounts of night.
When the Earth is in this position, the angle of the Sun is lower. The sunlight hits the Earth less directly in the Southern Hemisphere. When this happens, it causes cooler temperatures than other times of year when the sunlight hits the Earth more directly.
When it is Biderap season (January), the Southern Hemisphere is tilted towards from the Sun.
Since the Southern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, they would experience higher amounts of daylight and shorter amounts of night.
When the Earth is in this position, the angle of the Sun is higher and the sunlight hits the Earth more directly in the Southern Hemisphere, when the sunlight hits more directly, it causes warmer temperatures than other times of year when the sunlight hits the Earth less directly.  

Q2. Why do the warmer months in the United States occur at the same time as the cooler months in Australia and the cooler months in the United States align with the warmer months in Australia?


Student explanations should include the following:
Tilt results in differences in the amount of sunlight on different parts of the Earth’s surface at different times of the year.
Solar elevations are opposite in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres at the same time (July or January).
Light energy amounts reaching the surface are directly related to solar elevations:
higher solar elevation = higher light energy; and
lower solar elevation = lower light energy.
Temperature is directly related to light energy amounts reaching the surface.

Lesson 3 How can we explain the Sun’s path change over time?

-Changes in the length of a day and angle of elevation of the Sun in the sky occur in a regular, repetitive, and cyclical pattern.

-Earth’s axis is tilted at an angle and is pointed in the Northern hemisphere towards Polaris, called the "North star".

-As the Earth orbits around the Sun and is tilted on its axis, we observe changes in the amount of daylight and how high the Sun is in the sky over the course of the year.



Lesson 4

-How do these changes in sunlight impact us here on Earth?

-Solar elevation causes differences in the intensity of light reaching Earth’s surface throughout the year.

-Higher solar elevation results in more direct sunlight and greater light energy reaching Earth’s surface causing higher temperatures.

-Lower solar elevation results in sunlight being more spread out and less light energy reaching Earth’s surface causing lower temperatures.

-When the sunlight is direct on the top half of Earth (the Northern Hemisphere), it is at an angle in the Southern Hemisphere, and vice versa. This is one reason (in addition to corresponding changes to the amount of daylight hours) why the seasonal patterns are the opposite in places like Australia and South Africa compared to our area.
