##Student Instructions
Q1a. How would the amount of force on the head compare to the amount of force on the object it collides with in each system A, B, and C?


Q1b. Explain in words your choice in Q1a.


Q2a. Considering both objects that are about to collide in each interaction, which system would you predict to have the least amount of total kinetic energy in the system right before the objects collide? In each instance below, player #84 is running at a speed of 7 miles per hour. 


Q2b. Explain in words and pictures using the images below why the system you chose in 3a would have the least amount of total kinetic energy before the collision by comparing it with the two other systems.


Q3. Based on your answer in Q2b, which system should have the least amount of peak forces during the collision? Why?

Q6.  Suggest one or more ways we could use the materials from previous investigations to determine whether doubling the mass of a moving object or doubling its speed has a bigger effect on the amount of damage that occurs in a collision.

Criterion: Forces: Newton's 3rd law, Question 2a and Question 2b. Total points for this criterion is 3

Question 2a. How would the amount of force on the head compare to the amount of force on the object it collides with in each system A, B, and C?

Correct response is “A. In every collision, the peak force on the head would be the same as the peak force on the other object in the collision.”

(1 point for correct response)

Question 2b. Explain in words your choice in 2a.
Student responses should include these ideas:
+ You can’t have force on only one object when it makes contact with another object (forces come in pairs).
+ These contact forces are always equal, even if one object has more mass or more speed before they make contact in a collision.

(1 point for each idea, evidence or example mentioned, max 2 points)


Criterion: Forces and Kinetic Energy, Question 3a and Question 3b and Question 4. Total points for this criterion is 4

Question 3a. Considering both objects that are about to collide in each interaction, which system would you predict to have the least amount of total kinetic energy in the system right before the objects collide? In each instance below, player #84 is running at a speed of 7 miles per hour. 

Correct response is either “A. Player #84 running and heading the soccer ball that was kicked by another player” or “C. Player #84 running and hitting the ground”

(1 point for correct response)

3b. Explain in words and pictures using the images below why the system you chose in 3a would have the least amount of total kinetic energy before the collision by comparing it with the two other systems.

Student responses should include these ideas:
+ There are two objects in each system (A, B, and C) that will collide; the total Kinetic Energy is based on both.
+  In each system (A, B, and C) player #84 would have the same amount of Kinetic Energy because in each case it’s the same player with the same mass and speed.
+ The ground is not moving in system C, so it has no Kinetic Energy.  
+ There is a second object moving in A and B (a ball or another person), meaning each of these also have Kinetic Energy.  
+ So therefore, the total Kinetic Energy in system C should be lower than the other two systems.

(1 point for reasoning)


Question 4. Based on your answer in 3b, which system should have the least amount of peak forces during the collision? Why?
Correct response is C. 
(1 point for correct response)

Student responses should include these ideas:
+ Total peak force is related to the amount of total Kinetic Energy in the system.
+ So the system with the least Kinetic Energy (system C) should result in the least amount of peak force during the collision.
(1 point for reasoning)

Criterion: Structure of the Human Body, Question 5. Total points for this criterion is 4

Question 5. Write an argument: During a head collision, which system (A, B, or C) would be most likely to cause damage to some of the axons in the brain? Use ideas related to kinetic energy, peak forces, and breaking point for these structures in the brain and axons in your argument.

+ The collision in system B is more likely to cause damage to the brain because another person running is big and has a lot of mass, and mass is related to Kinetic Energy.
(1 point for identifying system B will cause most damage)

+ The axons will only break if the peak force exceeds their breaking point.
(1 point for identifying that long structure of axons make them break easily)

+ Mass is more important than speed for total Kinetic Energy
+ The peak force is related to the amount of total Kinetic Energy in the system before the collision.
+ So the system with more Kinetic Energy should result in a higher peak force during the collision.
+ This is why system B is more likely to cause damage to some cells in the brain in a collision.
(2 points for explaining the connection between kinetic energy, peak force, and breaking point and why brain damage occurs)
